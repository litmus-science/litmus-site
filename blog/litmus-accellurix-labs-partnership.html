Bridging Computational AI and Academic Cores: The Litmus Science × Accellurix Labs Partnership

Combining Litmus Science's AI-native lab execution with Accellurix Labs' core facility network — routing AI-designed candidates straight into university cores for fast, cost-effective validation, then feeding the results back into the model.

By Arun Nijhawan, Litmus Science & Alix McCloskey, Accellurix Labs

AI can propose hundreds of novel molecular candidates in seconds. Testing them still means finding a lab, scoping a protocol, and waiting — often weeks — for something that should take days. That gap, not model quality, is the actual bottleneck in AI-driven drug discovery today.

Litmus Science and Accellurix Labs have partnered to close it by routing AI-designed candidates directly into university core facilities. Litmus is the MCP-native execution layer: it captures candidate designs from AI models, translates them into standardized assay packages, and routes them across a provider network. Accellurix is the institutional gateway: pre-negotiated Master Service Agreements with R1 universities give direct access to 30+ core facilities — NMR, mass spec, cryo-EM, flow cytometry, SPR/BLI binding, and more — without a new contract per project.

The closed loop

AI proposes → Litmus ingests via MCP, plans, and quotes the study → Accellurix routes it to the right university core → Litmus feeds structured results back into the model.

How a Request Moves Through the Network

Consider an AI discovery startup validating 50 novel protein variants:

1. Programmatic ingestion

The startup's generative model outputs 50 candidate variants. Litmus captures the list via MCP and standardizes it into a machine-readable test package: express/purify 50 variants, SPR/BLI binding KD, solubility and aggregation.

2. Multi-provider benchmarking

Litmus compares execution options across commercial CROs, cloud labs, and Accellurix Labs' academic core network:

3. Execution and closed-loop feedback

The startup selects Core A for its combination of expert support and lower cost. Accellurix manages the SOW, logistics, and sample tracking directly with the core facility. When the assay completes, Litmus ingests the structured data back into the AI model to design the next round of candidates.

Why route to a university core at all

  • Cost — Academic cores run on non-profit, break-even recovery, not commercial markup, for early-stage, non-GLP screening.
  • Expertise — Requests are reviewed by the PhD faculty who run the core, not a standardized assembly line.
  • Instrumentation — Cores house federally-funded, specialized equipment — ultra-high-field NMR, cryo-EM — that most CROs can't justify buying.

Cores aren't a replacement for CROs or cloud labs — GLP/GMP work still routes to accredited providers. Litmus just gives biotechs visibility across all three lanes, so each experiment goes to whichever fits.
